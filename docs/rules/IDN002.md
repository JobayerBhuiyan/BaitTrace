# IDN002 Reply-To points at a different organisation

## What it detects

A `Reply-To` address whose registrable domain differs from the `From`
domain. A reply would be delivered somewhere the apparent sender cannot see.

## Why it matters

Redirecting replies is how a conversation is moved onto infrastructure the
attacker controls. The original message only has to look right; the payload
is the reply with credentials, a wire instruction, or a document.

The rule is weighted down when bulk-mail headers (`List-Unsubscribe`,
`Precedence: bulk`, and similar) are present, because this pattern is also
how legitimate newsletters work.

## Example

```
From: Microsoft Support <account@micros0ft-login.example>
Reply-To: Account Recovery <recovery@mail-secure.example>
```

A reply leaves `micros0ft-login.example` and lands at `mail-secure.example`.

## Known false positives

Extremely common in legitimate bulk mail: newsletters, ticketing systems, and
no-reply senders routinely redirect replies. Weighted low for this reason.
The benign corpus sample `newsletter.eml` exists to keep that weight drop
honest.
