# IDN005 Sender domain uses punycode or mixes scripts

## What it detects

The sending domain is internationalised (`xn--` / Punycode) or combines
writing systems within the name. Mixed script inside one label is the
stronger half of the signal; a purely punycode domain used by a non-English
organisation is the weaker half.

The decoded Unicode form is shown in the evidence so the analyst reads what
the recipient's client rendered, not the ACE string.

## Why it matters

Homograph attacks make one alphabet impersonate another. `аpple.com` with a
Cyrillic а renders as apple.com in most fonts. Punycode is how that name
travels on the wire. A reader who "checks the domain" still sees Apple.

## Example

```
From: Apple Support <billing@xn--80ak6aa92e.example>
```

The ACE label decodes to a lookalike of a brand name. Combined with IDN003
this is usually a high-confidence impersonation.

## Known false positives

Non-English organisations use internationalised domains legitimately. Mixed
script within one label is the stronger part of this signal; a single-script
IDN from a country-code TLD is often fine. Weight and confidence already
reflect that split.
